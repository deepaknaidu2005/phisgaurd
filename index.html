01 · Understand the Threat

Phishing is a form of social engineering in which an attacker tries to make a person reveal information or take an unsafe action. Modern scams can arrive by email, SMS, phone, social media, QR codes and fake websites.

📧

Email phishing

Fake invoices, account alerts, password resets and delivery notices.

📱

Smishing

SMS or messaging-app scams using urgency, payments, tolls or delivery problems.

☎️

Vishing

Voice calls that impersonate banks, support teams, officials or coworkers.

🎯

Spear phishing

Personalized messages aimed at a specific person or organization.

👑

Whaling

Targeted attacks against senior or high-value people.

🔳

QR phishing

QR codes that send victims to fake login or payment pages.

02 · Real-World Scam Patterns

Training scenarios below are fictional recreations based on documented scam patterns. No live malicious links, credentials or malware are used.

🏦 Bank Security Alert

Scenario: “Suspicious activity detected. Verify your account now.”

Red flags: urgency + unexpected login link + financial context.

Safe move: Open the official banking app/site yourself.

📦 Package Delivery Problem

Scenario: “Your parcel is on hold. Pay a small fee to reschedule delivery.”

Red flags: unexpected message + small payment + link.

Safe move: Check tracking through the courier's known official channel.

💳 Payment / Shopping Impersonation

Scenario: “A payment was made. Click here to cancel it.”

Red flags: panic + unknown transaction + login page.

Safe move: Open the service directly and check transaction history.

💻 Fake Tech Support

Scenario: A pop-up says your computer has a virus and gives a phone number.

Red flags: unexpected alarm + pressure to call + remote-access request.

Safe move: Close the page and use trusted support channels.

🎟️ Fake Invitation

Scenario: An event invitation asks for your email password to view it.

Red flags: unexpected invitation + credential request.

Safe move: Never enter a password on an unexpected invitation page.

🚨 Government / Toll Impersonation

Scenario: “Unpaid toll. Pay today or your vehicle registration may be affected.”

Red flags: authority + threat + deadline.

Safe move: Contact the agency using a website or number you already know is genuine.

💼 Fake Job / Recruiter

Scenario: A recruiter asks for a fee or sensitive identity information before an interview.

Red flags: unusual payment request + pressure + unverified recruiter.

Safe move: Verify the employer independently and never pay to get a job.

🤖 AI-Enhanced Impersonation

Scenario: A highly polished message, cloned voice or personalized profile appears to be from someone you trust.

Red flags: unusual request despite convincing language/voice.

Safe move: Use a second trusted channel to verify the request.

03 · Spot the Red Flags

Read the safe training message and identify the warning signs. This is a simulation only.

From: Account Security <security-alert@account-check.invalid>
Subject: URGENT — Your account will be suspended today

We detected unusual activity. To prevent suspension, confirm your password within 10 minutes using the verification button below.

🚩 Red flags found:
  • Urgency and threat of suspension
  • Unexpected password request
  • Suspicious sender/domain
  • Pressure to use a message link
Correct response: Do not click or reply. Open the official service through a known route and verify.

04 · Social Engineering Playbook

⚡ Urgency

Creates a deadline so you skip verification.

👔 Authority

Uses a boss, bank, teacher, police or support identity.

😨 Fear

Uses account closure, fines or security threats.

🎁 Reward

Uses prizes, refunds, discounts or jobs.

🤝 Trust

Uses familiar names, logos, contacts or relationships.

🧠 Curiosity

Uses unexpected files, invitations, photos or sensational claims.

05 · Fake Website Checklist

🔎 Domain: Is the actual domain expected? Watch for look-alike spelling.

🔐 HTTPS: Useful for encryption, but HTTPS alone does NOT prove a site is legitimate.

🔗 Link context: Were you unexpectedly sent here?

🔑 Credentials: Never provide passwords/OTPs to an unexpected page.

🧭 Independent navigation: For sensitive accounts, use a known bookmark or official app.

06 · If You Already Clicked

1

Stop

Do not continue entering information or communicating with the sender.

2

Protect

If you entered a password, change it from a trusted device/site and enable MFA.

3

Secure

If you downloaded something suspicious, disconnect as appropriate and follow trusted IT/security guidance.

4

Report

Report the message to your organization/service and the appropriate fraud-reporting authority.

5

Learn

Record what happened and review how the message bypassed your warning signs.

08 · Awareness Scorecard

🛡️ Layer 1: Pause before acting.

🔎 Layer 2: Inspect sender, domain and context.

☎️ Layer 3: Verify independently.

🔐 Layer 4: Use MFA and strong account protection.

📣 Layer 5: Report suspicious activity quickly.
